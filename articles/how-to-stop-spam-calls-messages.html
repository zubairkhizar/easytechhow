How to Stop Spam Calls and Messages on Your Phone

Short answer: turn on your phone's built-in spam and unknown-caller filtering first, then block and report the numbers that still get through, and never reply to, call back, or click links in suspicious messages. No single setting stops every spam call, because spammers constantly change numbers, but these steps together usually cut the volume dramatically within a week or two.

Why spam is getting worse

Spammers use software that dials thousands of numbers automatically and spoofs caller ID, so a call can look local even when it is not. If you answer once, your number may be marked as active and sold on, which is why answering and hanging up can actually increase calls. Text spam works the same way: a reply, even an angry one, confirms the number is live. The goal is therefore not to argue with spammers, but to make your number quiet, unresponsive and hard to reach for unknown callers, while keeping real calls from people you know working normally.

1. Turn on built-in spam protection

On Android, open the Phone app, go to Settings, then Caller ID and spam, and turn on See caller and spam ID and Filter spam calls. On iPhone, go to Settings, then Phone, and turn on Silence Unknown Callers, which sends numbers not in your contacts straight to voicemail. Also check your messaging app: Google Messages has Spam protection under Settings, and iPhone has Filter Unknown Senders under Settings, then Messages, which sorts unknown texts into a separate list. These built-in tools are free, need no extra app, and should be your first step.

2. Block and report, one number at a time

For numbers that still get through, block them in the Phone or Messages app and use the Report spam option where it appears. Blocking stops that exact number, and reporting helps your carrier and Google or Apple improve their filters for everyone. Do this consistently for a week: it feels tedious, but the filters learn from reports, and the volume usually falls. Do not block numbers you might need, such as delivery drivers or clinics that call from varying numbers — for those, save the number to contacts after the first legitimate call so future calls are not silenced.

3. Use your carrier's free tools

Many mobile carriers offer free spam blocking at the network level, which stops some calls before they reach your phone at all. Check your carrier's app or website for call protection, spam blocking or a do-not-disturb style service, and turn it on. Some carriers also let you report spam by forwarding a text to a short code, such as 7726 (SPAM) in several countries — check your carrier's instructions for your country before using it. Carrier tools work alongside your phone's settings, not instead of them, so use both.

4. Be careful with third-party blocker apps

Call-blocking apps can help, but they ask for a lot of access: your call history, contacts and sometimes your messages. Only install a well-known app from the official store, read what permissions it actually needs, and avoid apps that ask for access to your photos, location or social media accounts. A blocker app is a reasonable second step if built-in tools are not enough, but it is not a magic shield, and a poorly chosen app can become a privacy problem of its own. If an app starts showing ads on your lock screen or draining your battery, remove it.

5. Never engage with a suspicious message

Do not reply STOP to a message you did not sign up for, because for a scam message that reply simply confirms your number is active. Do not call back a missed call from an unknown international number, do not click links in delivery, bank or prize messages you were not expecting, and do not share verification codes with anyone who calls you — a real bank or service will never ask for a code by phone. If a message claims to be from your bank, delivery company or a government office, contact that organisation directly using a number from its official website, not a number or link in the message itself.

6. Reduce how widely your number is shared

Spam often starts when a number is leaked, sold or scraped from a form, a competition entry or a public post. Avoid posting your number publicly on social media or classified ads, use a separate number or a messaging username where a site offers one, and think twice before entering your number for a discount or a prize draw. If a business insists on a number for a one-off order, ask whether an email address will do instead. You cannot undo every past share, but slowing new ones down reduces the fresh supply of spam over time.

What about calls you actually need?

Silencing unknown callers can worry people who are expecting calls from a hospital, a school, a delivery driver or a new employer. The workaround is simple: ask expected callers to text first, save their number after the first call, and check your voicemail and your filtered messages folder once a day. Legitimate callers will leave a message or text; spammers almost never do. If you are job hunting or waiting for an important call, you can temporarily turn silencing off during the day and back on at night, or use Do Not Disturb with an exception for repeated calls.

Quick checklist

Turn on built-in spam filtering and unknown-caller silencing, block and report repeat numbers, enable your carrier's free protection, be selective about blocker apps, never reply to or click links in suspicious messages, and check voicemail and filtered messages daily. Give the filters a week or two to learn, and treat any sudden surge in calls after answering a spam call as a sign to go quiet again, not to answer more.

Frequently asked questions

Will blocking numbers stop all spam? No. Spammers keep changing numbers, so blocking alone never ends it. Blocking plus built-in spam filtering together usually cut the volume dramatically within a week or two.

Should I reply STOP to a spam text? No. Even an angry reply tells the spammer your number is live, which can bring more spam. Delete it, block the number, and report it instead.

Will silencing unknown callers block important calls? Calls from numbers not in your contacts go to voicemail, so check your voicemail regularly and save important numbers, such as your doctor or delivery services, in your contacts.

Are third-party blocker apps safe? Stick to the built-in tools first. If you do try an app, choose a well-reviewed one and check its permissions — an app that wants access to all your messages and contacts deserves a second thought.
