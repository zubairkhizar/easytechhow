How to Stop Your Phone from Overheating

A phone that feels warm after a long video call is normal. A phone that is too hot to hold comfortably, shuts itself down, or drains its battery in an hour is telling you something. The trick is not to panic and not to do anything drastic, like putting it in the freezer. Most overheating has a simple cause: a demanding app, a weak signal, a hot environment, or charging while working hard. This guide helps you cool the phone safely, find the cause, and stop it happening again.

A warm phone is normal, a hot one is not

Phones warm up during gaming, video calls, navigation and charging, especially in sunlight or a hot car. That warmth on its own is not a fault. Warning signs are different: the screen dims on its own, the phone shows a temperature warning, apps close unexpectedly, or the battery drops much faster than usual. If your phone regularly gets hot while doing nothing at all, or if the back is swelling, stop using it and get it checked. A swollen battery is a safety issue, not a settings problem, and it should not be ignored or squeezed back into shape.

1. Cool it down safely

Move the phone out of sunlight, take off a thick case, and stop heavy apps. Let it cool in the shade at room temperature. Do not put it in the fridge or freezer, because sudden cold can cause condensation inside the phone, which can damage it more than the heat did. Do not run it under cold water unless it is specifically rated for that, and even then, a gentle cool-down in air is safer. Give it ten to fifteen minutes. If it cools down and stays cool during light use, the cause was probably temporary.

2. Close heavy apps and stop background work

Games, video editing, navigation and video calls are the heaviest loads a phone carries. Close the ones you are not using, rather than leaving them running in the background. Check battery settings to see which apps have used the most power in the last day. An app you barely opened sitting at the top of that list is a suspect, and updating it, restricting its background activity, or removing it often fixes the heat. Also turn off background refresh for apps that constantly update when you do not need them to.

3. Check charging

Use a good-quality charger and cable, ideally the one that came with the phone or a reputable replacement. Cheap, damaged or frayed chargers can cause heat and are a fire risk, so replace them rather than tolerating them. Avoid using the phone heavily while it charges, especially for gaming or video calls, because charging and heavy use together generate far more heat than either alone. Do not charge the phone under a pillow, inside a bag, or in a hot car, where heat cannot escape. If the phone only gets hot while charging, try a different charger and cable before assuming the phone is faulty.

4. Reduce screen and network load

Lower the brightness a little, especially outdoors, where many people push brightness to maximum to fight glare. That maximum brightness is a major heat source. Turn off 5G if you do not need it in that moment, because searching for a weak 5G signal makes the phone work harder. The same applies to downloading large files on a weak signal: the phone boosts its radio power to hold the connection, which generates heat and drains the battery. Waiting until you have a stronger signal is not just faster, it is cooler too.

5. Update software and check battery health

Install system updates, which often fix overheating bugs and improve power management. In battery settings, check battery health if your phone shows it. A worn battery can run hot, hold less charge, and shut down unexpectedly, and at that point a battery replacement is a better fix than any setting. If overheating continues after all of this, or if it happens during light use with a healthy battery, contact the manufacturer or a reputable repairer, because it may be a hardware fault.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not put a hot phone in the freezer. Do not keep using it heavily while it shows a temperature warning, hoping it will sort itself out. Do not cover a charging phone with bedding or clothing. And do not ignore a swelling battery or a phone that smells odd when hot; in those cases, stop using it and seek advice rather than trying another tip from this guide.

When to get help

Get help promptly if the phone shuts down repeatedly from heat, if the battery is swelling, if it gets hot while idle, or if it stays hot after a software update and a restart. Those patterns point beyond settings, and a technician can check the battery and internal components safely.

Frequently asked questions

Is it normal for a phone to get warm while charging? Yes, mild warmth while charging is normal. It should not become uncomfortable to hold, and it should cool down once charging finishes.

Can a case cause overheating? A thick, insulating case can trap heat, especially during gaming or charging. Removing the case for a test is a quick way to find out.

Should I close all background apps every day? No. Modern phones manage background apps well. Only restrict or close apps that actually show up as heavy battery or heat users.

Does 5G make phones hotter? It can, especially on a weak signal, because the phone works harder to stay connected. Switching to a steadier network in that moment often helps.
