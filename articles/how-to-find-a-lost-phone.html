How to Find a Lost Phone

Short answer: if your phone is lost, act from another device. On Android, open Find My Device in a browser and sign in with the same Google account; on iPhone, open Find My on another Apple device or at iCloud.com and sign in with the same Apple ID. You will see the phone's last known location on a map, and you can make it ring even on silent, lock it with a message, or erase it if it looks stolen. A phone that is switched off only shows where it was last seen, so check as soon as you can.

Before you start: what you need

You need two things: another phone, tablet or computer with internet, and the password for the Google account or Apple ID on the lost phone. If you do not remember the password, recovering the account comes first, so sort that out before you ever lose a phone. You also need location to have been switched on at some point on the lost phone; without it, the map can only show a rough area. None of this needs a paid app — the built-in tools from Google and Apple do the whole job.

1. Find an Android phone with Find My Device

On any browser, go to Google's Find My Device page and sign in with the Google account that is on the lost phone. If you have more than one Android device on that account, pick the lost one from the list at the top. The map shows its location if it is online, along with its battery level. If it is offline, you will see the last location Google recorded, and the time it was recorded — check that time before you set off anywhere, because a location from three hours ago is a clue, not a live position. From the same page you can play a sound, secure the device, or erase it. Play the sound first if you think the phone is in the house or the car: it rings at full volume for five minutes even if the phone was on silent.

2. Find an iPhone with Find My

On another iPhone or iPad, open the Find My app and tap Devices, then tap the lost phone. On a computer, sign in at iCloud.com and open Find My. The map shows the phone's location, and you can play a sound, mark it as lost, or erase it. Marking it as lost is the step most people skip and then regret: it locks the phone with your passcode, shows a phone number and message on the lock screen so someone honest can call you, and switches on Activation Lock, which stops anyone else from wiping it and using it as their own. Newer iPhones can send their location for a while even after the battery dies, if that setting was switched on beforehand.

3. If the phone is nearby: ring it and retrace

Most lost phones are not stolen — they are in a jacket pocket, down the side of a sofa, or left in a taxi. Before you lock or erase anything, use the ring function and listen. Call the number from another phone as well, in case someone nearby picks up. Then retrace the last hour in order: the last shop, the last chair you sat in, the car, the bag you carried. Ask staff at the last place you visited — phones are handed in far more often than people expect. If the map shows the phone moving along a road, do not follow it yourself; contact the police with the location and the time.

4. If it looks stolen: lock it, then erase it

If the location is somewhere you have never been, or the phone is moving away from you, lock it first. On Android, choose Secure device: this locks the screen and lets you put a message and a callback number on it, and signs the phone out of your Google account. On iPhone, use Mark As Lost, which does the same job. Only erase the phone as a last step, when you are sure it is not coming back. Erasing deletes your photos, messages and accounts — and once it is erased, you can no longer track it. After erasing, change the passwords for your email and banking apps from another device, just in case.

5. Set this up now, on the phone you still have

The worst time to discover Find My was switched off is after losing the phone. On Android, open Settings, then Security, then Find My Device, and make sure it is on. On iPhone, open Settings, tap your name, then Find My, then Find My iPhone, and switch on Find My iPhone, Find My network, and Send Last Location. While you are there, write down your account password somewhere safe, and make sure your lock screen has a passcode — a phone with no passcode is an open phone for whoever finds it.

Common mistakes to avoid

The biggest mistake is erasing the phone immediately, which also erases your only way of tracking it. Another is signing in with the wrong account — a second Google account or an old Apple ID shows no devices at all, and people assume the tool is broken when it is just the wrong login. A third mistake is paying a website that claims it can track any phone number: no legitimate service does that.

Quick checklist

Sign in to Find My Device or Find My from another device with the right account, check the location and the time it was recorded, and ring the phone first if it might be nearby. If it is lost somewhere public, lock it and put a callback number on the screen. Erase it only when you are sure it is gone, then change your email and banking passwords.

Frequently asked questions

Can I find my phone if it is switched off? Only its last known location shows, with the time it was recorded. Newer iPhones can send a location for a short while after the battery dies, but an Android phone that is off cannot be tracked live.

Will the phone ring if it is on silent? Yes. Both Find My Device and Find My play a sound at full volume even when the phone is on silent or vibrate, for about five minutes. That is the fastest way to find a phone lost in the house.

Should I erase my phone straight away? No — lock it first. Erasing removes your ability to track it, so only erase it when you are confident it will not come back.
