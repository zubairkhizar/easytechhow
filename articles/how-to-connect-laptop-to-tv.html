How to Connect Your Laptop to a TV

Short answer: for films, presentations and everyday use, an HDMI cable is the simplest and most reliable way to connect a laptop to a TV. Plug the cable into both devices, switch the TV to the matching HDMI input, and set the laptop to duplicate or extend its screen. Wireless casting is convenient when it works, but it depends on your TV, your network and your laptop all supporting the same standard, so keep a cable as a backup for anything important.

What you need before you start

Check the ports on both devices first. Most modern laptops and TVs have HDMI, which carries picture and sound in one cable. Some slim laptops have only USB-C, in which case you need a USB-C to HDMI adapter or cable that supports video, not just charging — a charging-only adapter will not work. Older laptops may have DisplayPort, Mini DisplayPort or VGA, and older TVs may have VGA or component inputs, but HDMI is the option to prefer whenever it is available, because it is digital, carries sound, and needs no separate audio cable.

1. Connect with HDMI, step by step

Turn the TV on, plug one end of the HDMI cable into the laptop and the other into the TV, noting which HDMI port number you used. Use the TV remote to select that input, for example HDMI 1 or HDMI 2. On Windows, press Windows key + P and choose Duplicate to show the same picture on both screens, or Extend to use the TV as a second monitor. On a Mac, open System Settings, then Displays, and arrange the screens as you like. If the picture appears but the edges are cut off, look for an overscan or picture size setting on the TV and set it to Just Scan, Full or 1:1.

2. Get the sound to the TV

HDMI should carry sound automatically, but if the picture works and the sound still comes from the laptop, check the sound output. On Windows, right-click the speaker icon, open Sound settings, and select the TV as the output device. On a Mac, open System Settings, then Sound, then Output, and choose the TV. Also check the TV volume and make sure it is not muted or set to a different input's audio. If you are using VGA, remember that VGA carries no sound at all, so you will need a separate 3.5 mm audio cable from the laptop's headphone jack to the TV.

3. Fix a "no signal" message

No signal usually means the TV is on the wrong input, the cable is loose, or the laptop has not detected the TV yet. First, confirm the TV input matches the port you used. Then unplug and firmly reconnect both ends of the cable, and try a different HDMI port and, if possible, a different cable. Press Windows key + P again and choose Duplicate, or restart the laptop with the cable already connected, because some laptops only detect a TV at boot. Also update your graphics driver if the TV is detected but shows a blank or flickering screen.

4. Connect wirelessly, when it suits you

Wireless casting is handy for quickly sharing photos or a short video. On Windows, press Windows key + K, or open Quick Settings and choose Cast, then select your TV if it appears. On a Mac, use AirPlay from the menu bar or Control Centre and choose an Apple TV or an AirPlay-compatible TV. Chromecast works from the Chrome browser: click the three-dot menu, then Cast, and choose the TV or Chromecast device. Keep both devices on the same Wi-Fi network, keep the laptop reasonably close to the router, and close heavy downloads during casting, because wireless video is far more sensitive to a weak network than a cable is.

5. Choose the right display mode

Duplicate is best for presentations and for watching a film with others, because everyone sees the same thing. Extend is best for working, because you can drag windows onto the TV while keeping notes or controls on the laptop. Second screen only, which turns the laptop screen off, is useful for a clean film night or when the laptop screen is damaged. Whichever mode you choose, set the TV's resolution to its native resolution, usually 1920 by 1080 or 3840 by 2160, in Display settings, because a mismatched resolution makes text blurry and the picture look stretched.

6. Improve the picture and reduce lag

If video looks juddery or games feel delayed, switch the TV to Game mode, which turns off extra picture processing that adds lag. Use a wired HDMI connection rather than wireless for games or fast action, because wireless casting always adds some delay. Close unnecessary apps on the laptop, plug the laptop into power, and set its power plan to Best performance while connected. For films, turn off motion smoothing on the TV if faces look unnaturally smooth — most people prefer it off for movies, and it is usually found under Picture settings.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not buy the most expensive HDMI cable in the shop expecting a better picture: for normal lengths, a certified standard cable gives the same digital picture as a premium one. Do not force a connector into the wrong port, and do not use an adapter that is not rated for video. Avoid running a cable where it will be tripped over or pinched by a door, and do not leave a laptop balanced on the edge of a table just to reach the TV. If you regularly connect the same laptop to the same TV, a short cable and a fixed spot for the laptop will save you from most connection problems.

Quick checklist

Check the ports, use HDMI where possible, select the right TV input, press Windows key + P or use Displays on a Mac, set the TV as the sound output, and fall back to a cable whenever wireless casting stutters. Keep a spare cable for presentations, and test the setup once before an important meeting or film night, not five minutes before it starts.
