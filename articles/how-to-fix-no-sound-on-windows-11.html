How to Fix No Sound on Windows 11

Short answer: first check the obvious — volume up, not muted, and the right output device selected — because Windows often sends sound to a monitor, a TV or Bluetooth headphones you are not using. Then test with headphones, run the built-in sound troubleshooter, and only then touch drivers. Most no-sound problems are the wrong output device, not broken speakers, so work through the steps in order and stop as soon as sound returns.

1. Check volume and mute — in three places

Sound can be muted in more places than people expect, so check all three. First, the speaker icon in the taskbar: click it and make sure the volume slider is up and the speaker is not crossed out. Second, your keyboard: many laptops have a mute key or a volume wheel, and it is easy to press mute by accident — press the volume-up key a few times and watch the on-screen indicator. Third, the app itself: YouTube, Zoom and media players each have their own volume control and mute button, so a video can be silent even when Windows volume is fine. A practical example: sound works in Spotify but not in Chrome usually means Chrome's tab is muted — right-click the tab and check — not a Windows problem at all.

2. Choose the right output device

This is the single most common cause. Click the speaker icon in the taskbar, then click the small arrow next to the volume slider to see the output list. If it says your monitor, TV or a Bluetooth device, Windows is sending sound there instead of to your laptop speakers. Select Speakers, or your laptop's own audio device, and play something to test. This often happens after unplugging HDMI or turning off Bluetooth headphones — Windows does not always switch back on its own. To check the full list, go to Settings, then System, then Sound, and look under Output — your speakers should be the selected device, and the volume bar should move when sound plays.

3. Test with headphones

Plug in a pair of wired headphones or earphones and play a video. If you hear sound through the headphones but not through the speakers, the speakers or their setting is the problem, not the whole audio system — and you can keep using headphones while you fix it. If there is no sound through headphones either, the problem is in Windows settings or drivers. Try a different pair of headphones too, and check the headphone jack itself: dust or a loose connection can make sound cut out, and plugging the jack in and out a few times sometimes clears it.

4. Run the Windows sound troubleshooter

Windows 11 has a built-in troubleshooter that catches common faults. Go to Settings, then System, then Sound, then scroll to Advanced and click Troubleshoot under "Troubleshoot common sound problems", or go to Settings, then System, then Troubleshoot, then Other troubleshooters, and run Audio. It will check the output device, the driver, and the audio service, and it often fixes the problem itself — for example, restarting the Windows Audio service, which can stop after an update. Let it finish before trying anything else.

5. Update or reinstall the audio driver

A driver is the software that lets Windows talk to your sound hardware, and a bad update can break it. Right-click Start, choose Device Manager, expand "Sound, video and game controllers", right-click your audio device — usually Realtek Audio or similar — and choose Update driver, then "Search automatically for drivers". If Windows says the best driver is already installed but sound still does not work, try the opposite: right-click the device, choose Uninstall device, tick "Attempt to remove the driver" if it appears, then restart the laptop. Windows will install a fresh driver on startup.

6. Check Windows Update and restart

Sound problems often arrive with a Windows update and leave with the next one. Go to Settings, then Windows Update, and install any pending updates, then restart the laptop — a restart alone fixes a surprising number of sound faults, because it reloads the audio service and the driver from scratch. Also check the Windows Audio service: press Windows key + R, type services.msc, find Windows Audio, and make sure it is Running and set to Automatic.

When to get professional help

Take the laptop to a repair shop if there is no sound through speakers or headphones after a driver reinstall, if sound crackles or cuts out even after a clean restart, or if the speakers make a buzzing or rattling noise that suggests physical damage. Tell the shop what you tried — output device checked, headphones tested, troubleshooter run, driver reinstalled — so they start from the hardware instead of repeating your steps. If the laptop is under warranty, contact the maker first, because a speaker fault is usually covered and opening the laptop yourself could affect the warranty.

Quick checklist

Turn volume up and unmute in Windows, the app and the keyboard, select the correct output device, test with headphones, run the sound troubleshooter, update or reinstall the audio driver, and install pending Windows updates before restarting. If nothing works through any output, the fault is likely hardware.

Frequently asked questions

Sound works in one app but not another — is Windows broken? No. Each app has its own volume and mute settings, and Windows has a per-app volume mixer under Settings, then System, then Sound, then Volume mixer. Check the app's own volume first.

Why does sound go to my monitor instead of my speakers? HDMI carries sound as well as video, and Windows switches output to the monitor when you plug it in. It does not always switch back — change it manually from the speaker icon.

Should I install a driver updater program? No — use Device Manager or your laptop maker's official support page. Third-party updaters often install the wrong driver and bundle unwanted software.

My Bluetooth headphones connect but there is no sound — what now? Remove the headphones from Bluetooth settings, restart both the headphones and the laptop, then pair again. Also check the output device list — Windows may still be sending sound to the laptop speakers.
