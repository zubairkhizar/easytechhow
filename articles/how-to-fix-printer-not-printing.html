How to Fix a Printer That Won't Print

Short answer: most printers that will not print are stopped by one of five things: no connection, no paper, no ink, a stuck print queue, or a tired driver. Work through them in that order and you will find the cause in a few minutes. Start with the physical checks — power, cable or Wi-Fi, paper in the tray, ink levels — then look at the computer: is anything stuck in the queue, and does a restart clear it? Reinstalling the driver is the last step, not the first, and it fixes the cases where everything looks right but nothing comes out.

1. Check the basics: power, paper and ink

Switch the printer off and on again, and check the power cable is firmly in at both ends — a cable that looks connected but is loose is a classic cause. Look at the lights on the printer: a flashing ink or paper light is the printer telling you exactly what is wrong, so check the printer's screen for the message. Open the paper tray and check there is paper, that it is loaded straight, and that no crumpled sheet is jammed inside — pull any jammed paper out slowly, in the direction the paper normally travels, so you do not tear it and leave a piece behind. Then check the ink or toner levels: a printer that is out of black ink will often refuse to print even a black-and-white page.

2. Check the connection to your computer

If the printer is connected by USB, unplug the cable from both ends and plug it back in, and try a different USB port on the computer. If it is a Wi-Fi printer, check that the printer and the computer are on the same Wi-Fi network — this is the most common Wi-Fi printing failure, especially after a router restart, when the printer quietly joins the wrong network or none at all. On the printer's screen, open the Wi-Fi settings and check the network name. On the computer, open Settings, then Printers, and check the printer shows as Ready, not Offline. A quick test that saves time: print a test page from the printer itself, using its own menu — if that works, the printer is fine and the problem is the connection or the computer.

3. Clear a stuck print queue

Sometimes the printer is fine, but one failed job is blocking everything behind it. On Windows, open Settings, then Bluetooth and devices, then Printers and scanners, click your printer, and open the print queue. If you see a job sitting there with an error, cancel it, and cancel any other jobs behind it too. If the queue will not clear, restart the print spooler: this is the Windows service that manages printing, and restarting it clears the blockage. The simplest way is to restart the computer, which restarts the spooler with it. On Mac, open System Settings, then Printers and Scanners, click the printer, and open the queue to cancel stuck jobs the same way. After clearing the queue, try printing a single simple page first — one page of text — before sending the whole document again.

4. Restart both the printer and the computer

Turn the printer off, unplug it from the wall for a full minute, then plug it back in and switch it on. Unplugging matters: switching it off with the button alone leaves some printers in a sleep state, and a proper power cycle clears the error. While the printer is off, restart the computer too. When both are back on, print the test page again. This step sounds too simple to work, but it fixes a surprising number of printing problems — a printer that has been on for weeks can get into a confused state, and a computer that has been on for weeks can hold on to a broken printer connection. If you print from a phone as well, test from the phone too: if the phone prints and the computer does not, the problem is on the computer, not the printer.

5. Update or reinstall the driver

A driver is the small piece of software that lets the computer talk to the printer, and it is the cause when the printer is connected and ready but nothing prints, or when printing worked before a Windows or Mac update and stopped afterwards. First, check for an update: on the printer maker's website, find your exact printer model and download the latest driver for your version of Windows or Mac — do not download drivers from random driver websites, which are often bundled with unwanted software. If updating does not help, remove the printer from Settings, uninstall the driver, restart the computer, and add the printer again from scratch. After reinstalling, print a test page before you print anything important.

Common mistakes to avoid

The biggest mistake is buying new ink cartridges before checking the queue or the connection — most "out of ink" problems are actually a stuck job or a Wi-Fi problem. Another is forcing jammed paper out roughly, which tears it and leaves a piece inside that causes the next jam too. A third is printing the same document five times in frustration: each copy joins the queue behind the stuck one, so you end up with five copies at once when it finally clears.

Quick checklist

Check power, paper and ink, and pull out any jammed paper gently. Check the printer is on the same Wi-Fi network as the computer, or the USB cable is firmly in. Clear the print queue, restart both devices, and print a single test page. Only then update or reinstall the driver from the printer maker's own website.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my printer say it is offline? Usually it is on a different Wi-Fi network from the computer, the Wi-Fi dropped, or the USB cable is loose. Check the network name on the printer's screen, reconnect it, and restart both devices.

Why is my printer printing blank pages? The ink or toner may be empty, or the print heads may be clogged. Check the ink levels first, then run the printer's head-cleaning function from its menu or app.

How do I clear a print job that will not cancel? Open the print queue and cancel the job, then restart the computer — that restarts the print spooler and clears the stuck job.
