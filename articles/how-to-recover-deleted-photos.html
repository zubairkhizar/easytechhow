How to Recover Deleted Photos on Your Phone

Short answer: do not take new photos yet, then check your gallery trash, then your cloud backup trash, then any other device or app that may hold a copy. Most deleted photos stay in a trash folder for about 30 days, so a photo deleted today is usually recoverable in a few minutes, while a photo deleted months ago, or one that was never backed up, is much harder to get back.

First: stop overwriting the photo

When you delete a photo, your phone usually marks its storage space as free rather than wiping it straight away. New photos, videos and app downloads can take over that space, which is why recovery becomes harder the longer you wait. So pause for a moment: avoid taking burst photos, recording long videos or installing large apps until you have checked the places below. This single pause gives every later step a better chance of working.

1. Check the trash in your gallery app

On Android, open Google Photos or your phone maker's gallery app, tap Library, then Trash or Bin. On iPhone, open Photos, tap Albums, scroll down to Recently Deleted. If your photo is there, select it and tap Restore. It returns to its original album, usually with its date intact. Note the countdown shown next to the photo: that is how many days remain before it is deleted permanently. Restore the important photos first, not the whole folder, so you do not accidentally restore hundreds of unwanted images at the same time.

2. Check your cloud backup trash separately

Your gallery trash and your cloud trash are not always the same folder. If you use Google Photos, open photos.google.com on a computer and check its Trash as well, because a photo removed from the app may still be sitting there. iPhone users should check iCloud.com, then Photos, then Recently Deleted, and also check whether the photo is in a shared album or on another Apple device signed into the same account. A photo that is missing from one device is sometimes still present on an iPad, a second phone or a computer that has not synced the deletion yet.

3. Look in other apps and folders

Many photos live in more than one place without you realising it. Check WhatsApp, Telegram, email attachments and messaging apps, because a photo you sent to someone may still be in that chat. Check your Downloads folder, your screenshots folder and any folder created by a camera or editing app. On Android, use the Files app and search by the photo's date or file type. Also check a memory card if your phone uses one: remove it safely, and look at its DCIM folder on a computer before formatting anything.

4. Restore from a backup, if you have one

If the trash is empty, a backup is your next best option. On iPhone, check Settings, then your name, then iCloud, then Manage Account Storage, then Backups, to see when the last backup ran. Restoring a full device backup is a big step and it replaces current data, so only use it for photos that matter a lot, and back up your current phone first. On Android, check Google One backup or your phone maker's backup tool for the last backup date. Our guide to automatic phone backup explains how to set this up so the next deletion is not a crisis.

5. Try a recovery app — carefully

Recovery apps scan free storage for files that have not yet been overwritten. They sometimes work, especially for recently deleted photos on a memory card, but they are not magic, and they are not all safe. Only use a well-reviewed app from the official app store, never grant it access to your contacts or messages, and never pay before it shows you a preview of a recoverable photo. Avoid apps that promise to recover photos deleted a year ago or photos from a phone that has been factory reset: in most cases that data is gone, and the app is more interested in your data or your money than in your photos.

What not to do

Do not factory reset your phone to try to recover a photo, because a reset wipes far more than it restores. Do not root or jailbreak your phone for recovery unless you already know exactly what you are doing, because a mistake can make the phone unusable. Do not install five recovery apps at once: each install writes data to the same storage you are trying to recover from. And do not trust a website that asks you to upload your whole gallery or sign in with your cloud password in exchange for recovery — that is a common way to steal an account.

When a photo is probably gone for good

Be honest with yourself about the odds. If the photo was deleted more than 30 to 60 days ago, was never backed up, the phone has since been reset, and a lot of new photos have been taken since, recovery is unlikely. That does not mean you should give up after one check, but it does mean you should not spend money on expensive recovery services for a single casual snapshot. For professional work, irreplaceable family photos or legal evidence, a reputable local data recovery service is worth asking about before you try anything destructive, but get a clear price first.

Stop this happening again

Once you have recovered what you can, turn on automatic backup for photos and check that it actually ran. Keep the trash setting in mind: a photo is not truly gone until its trash countdown ends. Consider a second copy for your most important photos, such as a computer folder or an external drive, because one backup is good and two copies in different places are much safer. Also free up storage regularly, because a full phone is more likely to push you into deleting photos in a hurry — our guide to freeing up phone storage without deleting memories covers the safe way to do that.

Quick checklist

Stop taking new photos, check the gallery trash, check the cloud trash, search messaging apps and other folders, check other devices on the same account, restore from a backup only if the photo matters enough, and try a reputable recovery app only as a last step. Work in that order, and check each place before moving on, because the easiest recovery is usually the first one you find.

Frequently asked questions

How long do deleted photos stay in the trash? About 30 days in Google Photos trash and in the iPhone Recently Deleted album. Each photo shows a countdown, so restore the important ones first.

Can I recover a photo deleted months ago? Only if a copy exists somewhere else, such as a cloud backup, another device, or a message where you shared it. If it was never backed up and the trash has emptied, recovery is very unlikely.

Are photo recovery apps safe? Some are, some are not. Only use a well-reviewed app, check what permissions it asks for, and never pay before it shows you a preview of what it found. Check the trash and backups first — recovery apps are a last resort.

What if my backup was switched off? Check every other place the photo might be: another phone or tablet, your email, and chats where you sent it. A shared copy is often the easiest recovery of all.
