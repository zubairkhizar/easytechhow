How to Fix a Phone That Won't Charge

Short answer: test one thing at a time — a different cable, a different charger, a different wall socket, then a clean charging port — before assuming the battery or the phone is broken. In most cases the fault is the cheapest part of the chain, and replacing the phone or the battery first wastes money. Work through the steps below in order, and stop as soon as charging starts again.

Safety first

If your phone, cable or charger is hot, swollen, cracked, wet or smells burnt, stop using it immediately and do not plug it in again. A swollen battery is a safety risk, not a repair project: keep the phone away from heat, do not press or puncture it, and take it to a repair shop. Likewise, never charge a phone that has just been dropped in water until it is fully dry. A few hours of patience is far cheaper than a damaged port or a short circuit.

1. Try a different cable

Cables wear out inside long before they look broken, especially near the connectors. Borrow a cable that is known to work with another phone, and make sure it is the right type for your phone — USB-C, Lightning or Micro-USB — and pushed fully into the phone. If charging starts, your old cable is the problem. Cheap, uncertified cables can also charge slowly or stop working after a software update, so replace a suspect cable with a certified one from a reputable brand rather than the cheapest cable in the shop.

2. Try a different charger and socket

Next, try a different wall charger, then a different wall socket. Phone chargers fail, and so do power strips, extension leads and car chargers. If your phone charges from a computer's USB port but not from a wall charger, the charger is the suspect; if it charges from one socket but not another, the socket or strip is the suspect. Also check the charger's wattage: a very old 5-watt charger may charge a modern phone extremely slowly, which can look like it is not charging at all, especially while you are using the phone.

3. Clean the charging port — gently

Pocket lint is the most common hidden cause of charging problems. Shine a light into the port and look for packed fluff at the bottom. Turn the phone off, then gently loosen the lint with a wooden or plastic toothpick, never a metal pin, and blow out the loose dust. Do not push hard or scrape the contacts inside. After cleaning, plug the cable in again: it should sit noticeably deeper and feel snug. If the port itself is loose, bent or the cable wobbles even after cleaning, the port may need professional repair.

4. Restart the phone and check for software issues

A frozen charging process or a buggy update can stop charging even when the hardware is fine. Restart your phone, then plug it in and leave it for ten minutes without using it. Check for a software update, because makers sometimes fix charging bugs that way. Also look at battery health: on iPhone, go to Settings, then Battery, then Battery Health; on Android, check Settings, then Battery, or your phone maker's device care section. A battery at very low health may charge slowly and drain quickly, which is a different problem from not charging at all.

5. Try wireless charging, if your phone supports it

If your phone supports wireless charging, place it on a wireless charger and see whether it charges. If wireless charging works but cable charging does not, the fault is almost certainly the cable, charger or port, not the battery. This is a useful test, and it can also keep your phone usable while you wait for a port repair. Remove thick cases and metal accessories first, because they can block wireless charging, and centre the phone on the charging pad.

6. Check for water, damage and heat

Many phones refuse to charge when they detect moisture in the port, and that warning should be taken seriously. Let the phone dry naturally in a ventilated place; do not use a hair dryer on high heat and do not insert anything absorbent into the port. If the phone has been dropped, look for a cracked screen near the port or a bent frame, which can point to internal damage. If the phone gets hot while charging, unplug it, let it cool, and try again with a different charger — persistent heat is a reason to get the phone checked, not to keep trying.

When to get professional help

Book a repair if the port is physically damaged, the battery is swollen, the phone charges only at a certain angle, or nothing in this guide works. Before you go, back up your phone if it still turns on, and note what you have already tested, because a repair shop will ask. A port replacement is usually much cheaper than a new phone, and a reputable shop should give you a price before starting. If the phone is still under warranty, contact the maker first, because opening the phone elsewhere can affect warranty cover.

Make charging last longer

Once charging works again, protect it: use one good cable instead of many cheap ones, avoid yanking the cable out at an angle, keep the phone out of dusty pockets without a case, and avoid charging in very hot places such as a car dashboard in summer. Charge between roughly 20 and 80 percent for everyday use when you can, and do not leave the phone plugged in overnight on a damaged cable. These habits do not just prevent the next charging failure — they also help the battery itself last longer, as our laptop battery guide explains for the same reason.

Quick checklist

Check for heat, swelling or water damage first, then try a known-good cable, a different charger, a different socket, a gentle port clean, a restart, and wireless charging if available. Test one change at a time, and write down which change fixed it, so you do not buy a new battery for what was really a ten-minute cable problem.
