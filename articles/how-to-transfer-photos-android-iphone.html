How to Transfer Photos from Android to iPhone

Switching phones should feel exciting, not stressful, but photos are where most people get nervous. Years of family pictures, holiday shots and screenshots live on the old phone, and the fear is that one wrong tap will lose them. The good news is that moving photos from Android to iPhone is well solved now, as long as you pick the right method for your situation and check the result before deleting anything from the old phone. That last rule matters more than any app choice, and I will repeat it because it is the one step people skip.

The easiest method: Move to iOS, during setup

If you have not set up the iPhone yet, this is the simplest route. On the iPhone setup screen, when you reach Apps and Data, choose Move Data from Android. On your Android phone, download the Move to iOS app from the Play Store and follow the prompts. The iPhone will show a code, you enter it on the Android phone, and the two phones connect directly over Wi-Fi. Your photos, contacts, messages and some other data transfer across. Keep both phones plugged into power and close together during the transfer, and do not use either phone for anything else until it finishes. For a large photo library this can take a while, and that is normal. Trying to rush it by switching apps is the most common cause of a failed transfer.

A practical tip: if you have thousands of photos, start the transfer in the evening and let it run while you do something else. The process is boring by design, and watching the progress bar does not make it faster. If the transfer stalls, the usual fix is to restart both phones and start again, rather than trying to rescue a half-finished move.

If the iPhone is already set up

Many people only think about photos after they have already set up the new iPhone, and that is fine. You can still move everything easily. Install Google Photos on both phones, make sure backup is turned on for your Android photos, and wait until the backup finishes. Then sign in to the same Google account in Google Photos on the iPhone, and your photos will appear there. If you want them in the iPhone's own Photos app as well, you can download selected albums, but many people are happy to keep them in Google Photos and save local storage.

Another option is to use a computer as a bridge. Connect the Android phone, copy the DCIM folder, which holds camera photos, to the computer, then connect the iPhone and import those photos. This sounds old-fashioned, but for a very large library it is often the most reliable method, because it does not depend on your Wi-Fi speed or cloud storage limits. Just be careful to copy, not move, the files in the first pass, so the originals stay on the Android phone until you have checked the result.

Use a cable for large libraries

For thousands of photos, a cable transfer is faster and more predictable than Wi-Fi. Use a USB-C cable or an adapter, trust the computer on both phones when asked, and copy the photo folders directly. The transfer itself is straightforward, but the checking afterwards is where people slip up. Open Photos on the iPhone and look through a few albums and dates, not just the most recent pictures. Check a photo from last year, one from a holiday, and one screenshot. If those are all present, the transfer almost certainly worked. If a whole month is missing, you will spot it now, while the originals are still safe on the old phone.

What about videos, albums and edited photos?

Videos transfer the same way as photos, but they take much longer because the files are larger. If your transfer seems stuck, it is often a single long video holding things up, not a failure. Albums and folders do not always survive a move in the same form. Google Photos keeps albums you created there, but folders copied by cable may arrive as one large group, and you may need to reorganise them on the iPhone. Edited photos can also be a surprise: some methods transfer the edited version only, while others transfer the original as well. If you have carefully edited pictures, keep a backup of the originals on a computer before you move, just in case.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not delete photos from the Android phone the moment the transfer bar reaches 100 percent. Wait until you have checked albums on the iPhone and confirmed that your cloud backup, if you use one, is also complete. Do not factory-reset the old phone on the same day you transfer, for the same reason. And do not assume that a messaging app's photos moved with your camera photos; pictures saved inside chats often need a separate backup, which is a different setting in each app.

Final checklist

Pick the method that fits your situation, keep both phones charged, let the transfer finish, check albums by date, and only then think about clearing the old phone. Done in that order, moving photos is a routine job, not a gamble.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to buy anything to transfer photos? No. Move to iOS, Google Photos and a cable transfer are all free. You only need a cable or adapter if you choose the cable method.

Will my photo quality drop during transfer? It should not, as long as you transfer originals and do not choose a "storage saver" or compressed backup option that reduces quality. Check the backup quality setting before you start.

What if Move to iOS fails halfway? Restart both phones, make sure they stay close together and on power, and try again. If it keeps failing, switch to the Google Photos or computer method instead.

Can I transfer photos after setting up the iPhone? Yes. Google Photos or a computer transfer both work after setup, and you do not need to reset the iPhone.
