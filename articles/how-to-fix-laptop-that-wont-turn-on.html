How to Fix a Laptop That Won't Turn On

Short answer: first find out whether the laptop is truly dead or just showing a black screen. Check the charger and socket, look for any light or fan noise when you press power, and try a hard reset by holding the power button for 15 seconds with the charger unplugged. Most "dead" laptops are a faulty charger, a fully drained battery, or a display problem — all fixable without opening the machine. Work through the steps in order and stop as soon as it powers up.

First: is it dead, or is the screen black?

Press the power button once and watch and listen carefully for ten seconds. If you hear the fan spin, feel the laptop get warm, or see the keyboard backlight or a charging light come on, the laptop is actually turning on — the screen is the problem, not the power. Skip to step 5 for that. If nothing happens — no light, no sound, no vibration — the laptop is not getting power at all, and the fault is in the charger, socket, battery or power button. This distinction matters because it decides which steps are worth your time: a black-screen laptop needs a display check, while a silent laptop needs a power check.

1. Check the charger and the socket

Unplug the charger from the laptop and the wall, then plug it back in firmly at both ends — chargers often work loose at the brick. Try a different wall socket, one you know works because something else is running from it. If the charger's indicator light stays dark, the charger is probably dead or the socket is off. A burnt smell, frayed cable or bent connector means stop using it. If you can borrow an identical charger, try it: if the laptop powers on, a replacement charger is far cheaper than a repair.

2. Try a hard reset (power drain)

Unplug the charger and remove every accessory: USB drives, mouse, external monitor, docking station. If your laptop has a removable battery, take it out. Now press and hold the power button for a full 15 seconds. This drains leftover charge and can clear a state where the machine is stuck halfway between on and off. Plug the charger back in — with the battery still out, if you removed it — and press power once. If it starts, the problem was a stuck power state. Put the battery back only after the laptop is running.

3. Give a drained battery time

A battery that has been drained to zero does not always respond instantly. Plug the charger in and leave the laptop alone for at least 30 minutes before pressing power again. Some laptops show a charging light only after a few minutes on a truly empty battery, so a dark laptop in the first minute does not mean the charger is failing. If the charging light eventually comes on but the laptop still will not boot, leave it charging for an hour and try again — a weak battery can need that long before it has enough charge to start the machine.

4. Check for signs of life one more time

With the charger plugged in, press power and look for the small signs: a power LED, the Caps Lock light when you press it, the fan spinning for a second, or the hard-drive light blinking. Try pressing Caps Lock or Num Lock — if the light toggles, the laptop is on and the screen is at fault. Also listen for beeps: some laptops beep in a pattern when hardware fails, and the pattern means something specific — two short beeps and one long beep, for example, often points to memory. Note any beep pattern — a repair shop will ask.

5. If the screen stays black but the laptop is on

First try the brightness keys — on many laptops the screen can be turned down to black by accident, and the brightness-up key brings it back. Then try an external display: plug in a monitor or a TV with an HDMI cable and press the display-switch keys, usually Fn plus one of the F keys, to send the picture to the external screen. If the external screen shows your desktop, the laptop's own screen is faulty but the machine works and your files are safe. If it also stays black, the fault is deeper and needs a repair shop.

When to get professional help

Take it to a repair shop if there is still no sign of life after a known-good charger and a hard reset, if you smell burning or see swelling around the battery, if the laptop beeps repeatedly, or if it turns on but shows nothing on any screen. Tell the shop exactly what you tried — charger swapped, hard reset done, external monitor tested — so they do not charge you for repeating those checks. If the laptop is under warranty, contact the maker first.

Quick checklist

Decide first whether it is dead or just a black screen, then check the charger and socket, try a different charger if you can, do a 15-second hard reset, give an empty battery 30 minutes on charge, and test with an external screen. Note any lights, fan noise or beeps before you call a repair shop.

Frequently asked questions

My laptop worked yesterday — why not today? Usually a charger failed overnight, the battery drained to zero, or an update left the machine stuck. The hard reset in step 2 fixes the last of these.

Should I keep pressing the power button? No — press it once and wait ten seconds. Pressing it repeatedly can interrupt the startup.

Can a dead battery stop a laptop turning on even when plugged in? Yes, on some models a completely dead battery blocks startup until it has taken some charge, which is why step 3 says to wait 30 minutes.

Is it safe to open the laptop myself? Only if it is out of warranty, you can see the screws and the RAM, and you are comfortable doing it. If anything resists or you are unsure, stop — a repair shop visit is cheaper than a broken connector.
