How to Clear Cache on Your Phone

Short answer: cache is just temporary files your apps save so they load faster — old images, thumbnails and bits of web pages. Deleting it is safe: it never removes your photos, messages, accounts or passwords. On Android you clear it per app in Settings, and on iPhone you clear it inside each app or through Safari's settings. Only do it when an app misbehaves or storage is nearly full — the phone rebuilds the cache afterwards.

What cache is — and what it is not

When you open Instagram, YouTube or Chrome, the app saves copies of things it expects to need again: profile pictures, video thumbnails, map tiles, website layouts. The next time you open the app, it loads those saved copies instead of downloading everything again, which is why apps feel fast after the first launch. That saved data is the cache. It is different from app data: your login, settings, downloaded videos, saved chats and game progress are app data, and clearing the cache does not touch them.

1. Check how much space cache is using (Android)

Before deleting anything, look at what is actually taking up room, so you are fixing the real problem. On Android, open Settings, then Storage, and look at the breakdown. On Samsung phones it is under Settings, then Battery and device care, then Storage. You will see categories like Apps, Images, Videos and System. Tap Apps and sort by size: the biggest apps are usually the ones with the biggest caches — browsers, social media and video apps are the usual suspects. If one app is using several hundred megabytes, that is your target. If your storage is mostly photos and videos instead, clearing cache will only free a little, and our guide to freeing up phone storage is the better fix.

2. Clear the cache for one app (Android)

Go to Settings, then Apps, then pick the app that is misbehaving or hogging space — Chrome, for example. Tap Storage, then tap Clear cache. That is all: the button greys out or the cache figure drops to zero. Do the same for any other heavy app, one at a time. A practical example: if Chrome keeps showing an old version of a website or a page will not load, clearing Chrome's cache and then reloading the page usually fixes it, because the browser stops using its saved copy and downloads a fresh one.

3. Clear the cache for all apps at once (Android)

Older Android versions had a single "Cached data" button that cleared everything at once; most modern phones removed it. Samsung still offers something close: Settings, then Battery and device care, then Storage, then tap the Clean now option, which clears cached files and other junk in one pass. On other phones, Google's Files app does the same job: open Files, tap Clean, and accept the suggestion to delete junk files. Do not install a third-party "cleaner" app that promises to speed up your phone — many of them show ads, ask for unnecessary permissions and do nothing the built-in tools cannot do.

4. Clear cache on iPhone

iPhone does not give you a single clear-cache button, so you work app by app. For Safari, open Settings, scroll to Safari, tap Clear History and Website Data — this removes cached pages, cookies and browsing history, and signs you out of websites, so be ready to log in again. For other apps, open the app itself and look in its settings: many apps have a "Clear cache" option buried there. Spotify, for example, has one under Settings, then Storage. If an app has no such option, the only way to clear its cache is to delete the app and reinstall it — but first check whether the app lets you offload instead: Settings, then General, then iPhone Storage, then tap the app and choose Offload App, which removes the app but keeps its documents and data, so reinstalling restores it as it was.

5. When clearing cache actually helps

Clear cache when an app shows old content, a website loads the wrong version of a page, an app crashes on launch, or storage is nearly full. A shopping app still showing last week's sale price is almost certainly showing its cached copy — clearing it forces a fresh load. If the app misbehaves again within a day, the problem is the app itself: update it or report the bug instead.

Common mistakes to avoid

The biggest mistake is tapping "Clear storage" or "Clear data" instead of "Clear cache" — that logs you out of the app and deletes downloaded content, offline maps and saved settings. A third mistake is clearing cache obsessively, every day, expecting a faster phone. It has the opposite effect for a day or two: apps have to re-download everything, so they load more slowly and use more mobile data. Once a month, or when something is actually wrong, is plenty.

Quick checklist

Check Storage first to find the heavy app, clear its cache from Settings on Android or from inside the app on iPhone, and never tap Clear storage by mistake. For Safari, use Clear History and Website Data; for an app with no cache option, offload or reinstall it. If the problem comes straight back, update the app instead of clearing the cache again.

Frequently asked questions

Will clearing cache delete my photos or messages? No. Photos, chats and contacts are stored separately from the cache. Only the "Clear storage" button on Android removes app data, so leave that one alone.

Will I be logged out of my apps? Clearing cache alone does not log you out. Clearing cookies in a browser, or using Clear History and Website Data in Safari, does sign you out of websites, so keep your passwords handy.

How often should I clear cache? Only when an app misbehaves or storage is tight — roughly once a month at most. Daily clearing just makes apps slower, because they have to rebuild the cache each time.

Does clearing cache make my phone faster? It frees storage and can fix a glitchy app, but it does not speed up the processor or the network. A slow phone is usually caused by full storage or an old battery.
