How to Free Up Storage on Windows 11

Short answer: open Settings, then System, then Storage, and look at what is actually using the space before deleting anything. In most cases the biggest wins are Windows temporary files, apps you installed once and forgot, and a Downloads folder full of installers. Clear those three first, then check the Recycle Bin, move large videos to an external drive or cloud storage, and turn on Storage Sense so Windows tidies up by itself in future.

1. See what is using the space

Click Start, then Settings, then System, then Storage. Windows shows a bar for your main drive and breaks it down into Apps, Temporary files, Documents, Pictures, and Other. Click each category to see the detail: Apps lists every installed program by size, Temporary files shows update leftovers and the Recycle Bin, and Documents shows your largest folders. A common surprise is a single game taking 60 or 80 gigabytes, or a video-editing app keeping gigabytes of preview files. Spend two minutes here — guessing and deleting random files is how people lose things they needed, while this screen shows you exactly where the space went.

2. Delete temporary files safely

On the Storage page, click Temporary files. Windows lists what it can safely remove: Windows Update Cleanup, temporary internet files, delivery optimisation files, and the Recycle Bin contents. Tick the boxes you want — update cleanup and delivery optimisation files are safe to remove, because Windows has already finished with them — and click Remove files. The Recycle Bin entry is worth checking first: open the Recycle Bin from the desktop and make sure nothing in there is something you deleted by mistake, because emptying it here is permanent. Do not tick Downloads here unless you have checked the folder first — it often holds files you want to keep.

3. Uninstall apps you do not use

Back on the Storage page, click Apps, then Installed apps, and sort the list by size. Go through it honestly: that photo editor you used once, the game you finished a year ago, the three different video players, the printer software for a printer you no longer own. Click the three dots next to an app and choose Uninstall. Large games and creative apps are the biggest wins — removing one unused game can free more space than a month of deleting small files. If you are unsure about an app, leave it: anything with Microsoft, Intel, Realtek or your laptop maker's name in it is usually a driver or system component, and removing drivers causes more problems than the space is worth.

4. Clean the Downloads folder and the desktop

Open File Explorer and look at Downloads sorted by size. You will usually find old setup files, duplicate downloads of the same installer, and large attachments you opened once. Delete the installers for programs you already installed — you can download them again if you ever need them — and move anything you want to keep into Documents or Pictures, where it belongs. The desktop deserves the same check: every file on the desktop is stored on your main drive, and a desktop full of video files is a common hidden cause of a full drive. Move those videos to a proper folder or an external drive instead of leaving them on the desktop.

5. Move large files off the main drive

Videos, photo collections and old project folders are often too big to delete but do not need to live on your main drive. Copy them to an external hard drive or a USB drive first, then verify the copy opens — actually open one video from the external drive before deleting the original. Cloud storage works too: OneDrive's Files On-Demand keeps files in the cloud with only a small placeholder on your PC — right-click a OneDrive folder and choose "Free up space". Whichever route you pick, the rule is the same: copy, verify, then delete — never delete first.

6. Turn on Storage Sense

Storage Sense is Windows 11's built-in automatic cleanup, and it stops the drive filling up again. On the Storage page, turn Storage Sense on, then click it to set the schedule: run it every month, delete temporary files automatically, empty the Recycle Bin after 30 days, and clean the Downloads folder only if you are comfortable with that — many people prefer to leave Downloads untouched and clean it by hand. It is the single best step for keeping a small drive — a 256 GB laptop drive, for example — usable without constant manual cleaning.

What not to delete

Do not delete anything inside the Windows folder, the Program Files folders, or any folder you do not recognise — those hold the operating system and your programs, and deleting them can break the laptop. Do not use aggressive third-party "PC cleaner" programs that promise to free gigabytes: they often delete browser passwords, saved sessions and useful caches, and some bundle unwanted software of their own.

Quick checklist

Check Storage to find the real culprit, remove temporary files, uninstall the biggest unused apps, clean Downloads and the desktop, move large videos to an external drive or OneDrive after verifying the copy, and turn on Storage Sense for monthly automatic cleanup. If the drive is still nearly full after all that, the honest answer is a bigger drive or an external one — not more deleting.

Frequently asked questions

How much free space should I keep? Aim for at least 10 to 20 gigabytes free. Below that, Windows Update can fail and apps slow down.

Is it safe to delete everything in Temporary files? Yes, except check the Downloads entry first — it can contain files you want to keep. Tick the rest without worry.

Why is my drive full when I hardly have any files? Usually it is apps and games, update leftovers, or the Recycle Bin. The Storage breakdown shows the answer in under a minute.

Does Storage Sense delete my personal files? No. It removes temporary files, Recycle Bin contents older than your chosen period, and OneDrive files already stored in the cloud. It does not touch your Documents, Pictures or Desktop files.
