How to Secure Your WhatsApp Account

WhatsApp accounts are a popular target for hijacking, because taking over one account gives an attacker access to your contacts, your groups, and your conversations. The attack usually starts with a verification code trick, not with hacking. These steps close that door, and they take about five minutes in total.

How WhatsApp accounts get hijacked

The most common attack is simple social engineering. Someone messages you pretending to be a friend or a support agent, and asks you to share a six-digit code that just arrived by SMS. That code is actually the code to register your WhatsApp number on their phone. Once you share it, your account moves to their device and you are locked out. Another route is a linked device left logged in on a shared computer, or a weak phone lock screen that lets someone read your messages. Understanding these routes matters, because every step below blocks one of them directly.

1. Turn on two-step verification now

This is the single most important step. In WhatsApp, go to Settings, then Account, then Two-step verification, and tap Enable. Choose a six-digit PIN you will remember, and add an email address for recovery. From now on, registering your number on any new phone requires this PIN as well as the SMS code, so an attacker who tricks you into sharing the SMS code still cannot take over your account. Do not use a PIN like your birth year or 123456, and do not share it with anyone, even someone claiming to be from WhatsApp support. WhatsApp will never ask you for this PIN.

2. Never share a verification code with anyone

Make this a hard rule: a six-digit code sent to you by SMS is yours alone. No friend, no bank, and no support agent will ever legitimately need it. If someone asks for it, assume the account is compromised, even if the message looks like it came from a contact, because that contact's account may itself have been taken over. If you already shared a code, act immediately: try to re-register WhatsApp on your own phone, turn on two-step verification, and warn your contacts not to trust messages from your account until you confirm you are back in control.

3. Check your linked devices regularly

WhatsApp lets you stay logged in on up to four other devices, such as a computer or tablet. Open Settings, then Linked Devices, and review the list. If you see a device or browser you do not recognise, or a session from a place you have never been, tap it and choose Log out. Also log out of sessions on shared or public computers as soon as you finish using them, rather than leaving them connected. Checking this list once a month takes thirty seconds, and it catches the quietest form of account misuse: someone reading your messages from a device you forgot about.

4. Lock WhatsApp and your phone properly

Your phone's lock screen is WhatsApp's first line of defence. Use a strong PIN, pattern, password, or biometric lock, not a simple swipe. On top of that, you can lock WhatsApp itself: on iPhone, go to Settings, then Privacy, then Screen Lock, and turn on Require Face ID or Touch ID. On Android, use your phone's app lock feature if available, or a trusted app locker from the Play Store. Also turn off message previews on the lock screen under notification settings, so a stranger holding your phone cannot read incoming messages without unlocking it.

5. Tighten your privacy settings

Under Settings, then Privacy, review who can see your profile photo, About info, Last Seen, and Status. Setting these to My Contacts instead of Everyone reduces the information strangers can gather about you, which makes impersonation attacks harder. You can also control who can add you to groups: set Groups to My Contacts, so random numbers cannot add you to spam or scam groups. Read receipts and live location sharing deserve a look too: turn off live location sharing unless you are actively sharing it with someone you trust, and check that it has actually stopped afterwards.

6. Turn on disappearing messages for sensitive chats

Disappearing messages reduce the damage if an account is ever compromised, because old messages are simply not there to read. You can set a default timer under Settings, then Privacy, then Default message timer, choosing 24 hours, 7 days, or 90 days for new chats. For existing sensitive chats, open the chat, tap the contact name, and set disappearing messages there. Note that disappearing messages do not stop someone from taking a screenshot or photographing the screen, so treat them as damage limitation, not as a guarantee of secrecy.

7. Back up chats safely, and encrypt the backup

WhatsApp chat backups on Google Drive or iCloud are a valuable target, because they contain your message history. Turn on end-to-end encrypted backup under Settings, then Chats, then Chat backup, then End-to-end encrypted backup, and set a password or 64-digit key. Store that password in a password manager, because losing it means losing access to the backup. Without encryption, a backup is protected only by your cloud account's security, which is weaker than WhatsApp's own encryption. This step is easy to skip and important to do.

8. Keep WhatsApp and your phone updated

Security fixes arrive in app updates, and WhatsApp is updated frequently for exactly this reason. Turn on automatic app updates in your app store, and install phone system updates promptly. Older versions of WhatsApp have had serious vulnerabilities that allowed attacks without any action from the user, and those are fixed by updating. If you sideload WhatsApp from outside the official app store, stop: unofficial versions can contain malware and are not supported, and they put your account and messages at risk.

Final checklist

Enable two-step verification with a PIN only you know, and never share a verification code with anyone. Check linked devices monthly, lock your phone and WhatsApp, and set profile and group privacy to contacts only. Use disappearing messages for sensitive chats, encrypt your chat backup, and keep WhatsApp updated from the official store. These eight steps close the common attack routes, and they take less time than recovering a hijacked account.

Frequently asked questions

What if I forget my two-step verification PIN? You can reset it using the recovery email you added when you set it up. If you did not add an email, you will have to wait seven days before you can re-register without the PIN, which is why adding the email matters.

Does two-step verification stop someone reading my messages on a linked device? No, it protects registration of your number on a new phone. Linked devices are a separate risk, which is why checking the linked devices list regularly is its own step.
