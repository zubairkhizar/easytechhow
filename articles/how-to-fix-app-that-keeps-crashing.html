How to Fix an App That Keeps Crashing on Your Phone

Short answer: force-stop the app, restart your phone, then check for an app update and free up some storage — those four steps fix most crashes. If the app still crashes, clear its cache on Android, and only reinstall it as a last resort, after checking that your chats, photos or game progress are backed up. The order matters: reinstalling first can wipe data that a simple restart would have saved.

What a crash usually means

When an app closes the moment you open it, or freezes and returns you to the home screen, something in the app's own state is broken — a corrupted saved file, a half-finished update, or a conflict with a recent phone update. It rarely means your phone is broken. The pattern tells you a lot: an app that crashes only when you open one particular chat, photo or file has a problem with that item; an app that crashes on every launch has a problem with itself; and every app crashing points to the phone — usually storage so full that apps have nowhere to write temporary files. Keep that pattern in mind as you work through the steps.

1. Force-stop the app and restart your phone

On Android, open Settings, then Apps, pick the app, and tap Force stop. On iPhone, swipe up from the bottom (or double-press the home button on older models) to open the app switcher, then swipe the app's card up and off the screen. Then restart the phone properly — hold the power button and choose Restart, rather than just locking and unlocking it. This clears the app from memory completely and gives it a clean start. If the app works now, you are done — do not keep fixing what is already fixed.

2. Check for an app update

Open the Play Store on Android or the App Store on iPhone, search for the app, and see whether an Update button is showing. Developers fix crash bugs in updates all the time, and a crash that started right after a phone software update is often fixed by an app update released a few days later. Also check for a phone system update in Settings. If the app has no update available, read the recent reviews in the store — if dozens of people are reporting the same crash this week, the developer already knows, and your best move is to wait for the next update rather than reinstalling.

3. Free up storage space

Apps need free space to run: when storage drops below roughly a gigabyte, apps start crashing, photos stop saving, and updates fail. Check it under Settings, then Storage, on both Android and iPhone. If you are nearly full, delete a few large videos you have already backed up, remove apps you never open, or clear the cache of your biggest apps — our guide to clearing cache on your phone walks through that step by step. Freeing even half a gigabyte is often enough for the app to run normally again, and it also makes the whole phone feel faster.

4. Clear the app's cache (Android)

On Android, go to Settings, then Apps, pick the app, tap Storage, then tap Clear cache. This removes the app's temporary files without touching your login or saved content, and it fixes crashes caused by a corrupted cached file — for example, a social media app that crashes every time it tries to load a particular image from its cache. On iPhone there is no per-app cache button, so check inside the app's own settings first — some apps offer a clear-cache option there — and if there is none, skip to the next step. Never tap "Clear storage" or "Clear data" on Android unless you are ready to log in again and lose the app's downloaded content; that button is for a different problem.

5. Check the app's permissions

An app that needs the camera, microphone or location and does not have permission can crash when it tries to use that feature. On Android, go to Settings, then Apps, pick the app, then Permissions, and allow what it needs — a camera app without camera permission is the classic case. On iPhone, go to Settings, scroll down to the app, and check the same list. If you denied a permission during setup and the app started crashing afterwards, allowing it is the fix.

6. Reinstall the app — carefully

If nothing else works, uninstall and reinstall. First, make sure your data is safe: back up chats, sync photos, and check game progress is tied to an account. Then delete the app, restart the phone, and install a fresh copy from the official store — never from a random website offering an APK file, which can be unsafe. If a freshly installed app still crashes immediately, the problem is almost certainly the phone's software or the app's latest version itself, not your data.

Quick checklist

Force-stop the app, restart the phone, update the app, free up storage, clear the cache on Android, check permissions, and only then reinstall — after backing up chats and photos. If everyone else reports the same crash, wait for the developer's fix instead of reinstalling again.

Frequently asked questions

Will clearing cache delete my chats or photos? No — it only removes temporary files. Clearing storage or data is the button that deletes app content and logs you out.

Why does only one app crash while the rest work fine? Because the fault is inside that app — a bad update, a corrupted cache file, or a permission problem — not in your phone. That is actually good news: the fix is limited to that app.

Should I delete and reinstall the app first? No — reinstalling is the last step, because it can wipe data a restart or an update would have preserved.

The app crashes only when I open one photo or chat — what does that mean? That specific item is corrupted. Try opening it on another device or from the app's web version, and delete or re-download that one item instead of the whole app.
