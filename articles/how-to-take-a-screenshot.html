How to Take a Screenshot on Any Device

Short answer: on Windows press Windows key + Shift + S, on Mac press Command + Shift + 4, on Android press Power + Volume Down together, and on iPhone press Side button + Volume Up together. Each method saves the picture in a different place, so the second half of this guide is knowing where to find it afterwards. Once you know your device's shortcut, the rest — selecting an area, editing, sharing — is the same idea everywhere.

1. Screenshot on Windows 11

The most useful shortcut is Windows key + Shift + S. The screen dims, a small toolbar appears at the top, and you can drag to select just the part you want. Release the mouse and the screenshot is copied to the clipboard — paste it straight into WhatsApp, Word or an email with Ctrl + V. If you want it saved as a file instead, press Windows key + Print Screen together: the screen flashes briefly and the image is saved in Pictures, then Screenshots. Windows also has the Snipping Tool app: search for it in the Start menu, click New, select an area, and use its pen and highlighter to mark things up before saving.

2. Screenshot on Mac

On a Mac, Command + Shift + 3 captures the whole screen, and Command + Shift + 4 lets you drag to select an area — press Space after that shortcut and the cursor turns into a camera that captures a single window instead. By default the file appears on the desktop with a name starting "Screenshot" and the date and time. If you want more control, press Command + Shift + 5: a toolbar appears with options for the whole screen, a window, or a selected area, plus a timer and a choice of where to save.

3. Screenshot on Android

On almost every Android phone, press the Power button and Volume Down button at the same time and hold them for a second. The screen flashes, a small preview appears in the corner, and you can tap it to crop or draw on the image before it is saved. Samsung phones offer a second way: swipe the edge of your hand across the screen from one side to the other, if that gesture is turned on in Settings, under Advanced features, then Motions and gestures. Screenshots are saved in the Gallery or Google Photos, in a folder called Screenshots.

4. Screenshot on iPhone

On iPhones with Face ID, press the Side button and Volume Up button together and release quickly — the screen flashes white and a thumbnail appears in the bottom-left corner. Tap the thumbnail to crop, add text or sign the image; swipe it away to save it untouched. On iPhones with a Home button, press the Home button and the Side button together instead. Screenshots go to Photos, in the Screenshots album under Albums, then Media Types.

5. Take a scrolling screenshot of a whole page

Sometimes what you need is longer than one screen — a full chat, a long article, a receipt. On Android, take a normal screenshot and tap the "Capture more" or scroll button that appears on the preview; the phone scrolls down and stitches the images together. On Samsung it is called Scroll capture. On iPhone, take a screenshot in Safari, tap the thumbnail, choose Full Page, and save it as a PDF. On Windows, use your browser: in Chrome or Edge, press Ctrl + Shift + I, then Ctrl + Shift + P, type screenshot, and choose Capture full size screenshot.

6. Find, edit and share your screenshot

Knowing where screenshots live saves more time than learning the shortcut. On Windows, check Pictures, then Screenshots — unless you used Windows + Shift + S, in which case the image is only on the clipboard until you paste it somewhere. On Mac, look on the desktop. On Android and iPhone, look in the Photos or Gallery app under the Screenshots album. Before sharing a screenshot, crop out anything private: notification bars can show your name, battery and signal, and the top of a chat screenshot can reveal a phone number.

Common mistakes

The most common mistake is taking the screenshot and then not being able to find it — remember that the clipboard shortcuts do not save a file at all. Another is pressing the buttons too slowly on a phone, which locks the screen or changes the volume instead of capturing anything; press them together, quickly. And if a screenshot comes out black — usually when capturing a video or a banking app — that is the app blocking screenshots on purpose for privacy or copyright reasons, not a fault with your phone.

Quick checklist

Windows: Windows + Shift + S for a selection, Windows + Print Screen to save a file. Mac: Command + Shift + 4 for a selection, Command + Shift + 3 for everything. Android: Power + Volume Down. iPhone: Side + Volume Up, or Home + Side on older models. For a long page, use the scrolling or full-page option, and crop before you share.

Frequently asked questions

Where did my screenshot go? On Windows it is in Pictures, then Screenshots, or only on the clipboard if you used Windows + Shift + S. On Mac it is on the desktop. On Android and iPhone it is in the Photos app, in the Screenshots album.

Why is my screenshot black? Some apps — banking apps, streaming apps, and apps playing protected video — block screenshots. The black image means the app stopped the capture on purpose; there is no setting that overrides it.

Can I screenshot a whole web page? Yes. On iPhone use the Full Page option in Safari, on Android use the scroll option after taking a screenshot, and in Chrome or Edge on a computer use the developer-tools capture command.

How do I record my screen instead? On Windows press Windows + G and use Game Bar, on Mac press Command + Shift + 5 and choose Record, and on phones use Screen Record in quick settings or Control Centre.
