How to Record Your Screen on Any Device

Short answer: every modern device already has a screen recorder built in, so you do not need to download anything. On Windows 11 press Windows key + Alt + R, on Mac press Command + Shift + 5, on iPhone use the Screen Recording button in Control Centre, and on Android swipe down twice and tap Screen record. Start the recording, do the thing you want to show, then stop it — the video is saved automatically. The only part people forget is the sound: check before you start whether the microphone is on, or your recording will be silent.

Before you record: two quick checks

First, close anything private — extra browser tabs, message notifications, your email inbox — because the recorder captures everything on screen, including pop-ups. Switch on Do Not Disturb so a message does not flash across the middle of your recording. Second, decide whether you need sound. If you are showing someone how to do something, your voice matters, so the microphone must be on. If you are recording a clip playing on the device, you need the system sound instead. Check the setting before you press record, not after a ten-minute take.

1. Record on Windows 11

Press the Windows key + Alt + R to start recording the window you are in — a small bar appears showing the recording time. Press the same keys again, or click the stop button, to finish. The video is saved in Videos, then Captures. If that shortcut does nothing, open the Xbox Game Bar with Windows key + G, click the record button, and check that Game Bar is switched on in Settings, then Gaming, then Game Bar. One limit worth knowing: Game Bar records one app window at a time, not the whole desktop, so if you switch apps mid-recording, the recording keeps showing the first window. For a full-desktop recording, use the Snipping Tool instead: open it, switch to video mode, select the area, and press record.

2. Record on Mac

Press Command + Shift + 5 to open the screenshot toolbar, then choose whether to record the entire screen or just a selected part. Click Options first to pick where the video is saved, whether the microphone is on, and whether to show a five-second countdown so you have time to get ready. Click Record, do what you need to show, then click the stop button in the menu bar at the top of the screen. The video is saved to the location you chose — Desktop by default — and a small thumbnail appears in the corner for a few seconds, which you can click to trim the start and end. If you only need a quick clip of one app, recording the whole screen and trimming it later is usually faster than fiddling with the selection box.

3. Record on iPhone

Open Settings, then Control Centre, and add Screen Recording if it is not already there. Then swipe down from the top-right corner to open Control Centre, and tap the record button — a three-second countdown starts. To record with your voice, press and hold the button instead of tapping it, and switch the microphone on. The red bar or red clock at the top of the screen shows you are recording. Tap it and choose Stop when you are done, and the video appears in Photos. A practical example: if you are recording a tutorial for a family member, hold the phone steady, speak slowly, and pause a second between steps — it is much easier to follow than a rushed recording with no gaps.

4. Record on Android

Swipe down from the top of the screen twice to open the full quick settings panel, and tap Screen record. On some phones it is under a pencil or edit button the first time, because it is not on the panel by default. Before it starts, you can choose whether to record sound — from the microphone, from the device, or both — and whether to show your touches on screen, which is useful for tutorials. Tap Start, and a small floating button lets you stop the recording. The video is saved in your gallery, usually in a Screen recordings album. If your phone is a Samsung, the same tool is in the same place; if it is a Xiaomi or Oppo, the recorder is in the same quick settings panel but may be called Screen Recorder instead.

5. Make the recording worth watching

A good screen recording is short and planned. Before you press record, write down the three or four steps you are going to show, and close every app you do not need. Record in landscape if the video will be watched on a computer, and keep the phone still — a wobbly recording is hard to follow. After recording, trim the dead seconds at the start and end: Photos on iPhone, Google Photos on Android, and the Photos app on Windows and Mac all have a simple trim tool. Finally, watch the recording once before you send it: check the sound is there, check no password or private message is visible, and check the file is not so large that it will not send.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most common mistake is recording with the microphone off and only realising at the end, when the whole explanation is missing. Another is leaving notifications on, so a personal message appears in the middle of the video. A third is recording the wrong window on Windows — Game Bar only records one app, so switching apps mid-recording leaves you with a video of the wrong thing.

Quick checklist

Close private tabs and switch on Do Not Disturb, check whether you need microphone or system sound, and pick the right tool for your device. Record the steps in order, stop, trim the ends, and watch it once before sharing.

Frequently asked questions

Where does my screen recording go? On Windows it goes to Videos, then Captures; on Mac to the location you chose, usually Desktop; on iPhone and Android it goes to Photos or your gallery, in a Screen recordings album.

Why does my recording have no sound? The microphone or system sound was switched off when you started. On iPhone, press and hold the record button and turn the microphone on; on Android, pick the sound source before you tap Start.

Can I record a video call? Yes, technically — but tell the other people first and get their agreement. Recording someone without their knowledge is rude, and in some places it breaks the law.
