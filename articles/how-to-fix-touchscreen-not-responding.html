How to Fix a Touchscreen That Is Not Responding

Short answer: a touchscreen that stops responding is usually a simple problem, not a dead phone. Start with the easy fixes: clean the screen, take off the case and screen protector, and force-restart the phone. If only one app is frozen, the app is the problem, not the screen. Work through the steps in order — most people fix it in the first three, and a repair shop is only needed when a whole area of the screen stays dead after a restart.

First: what kind of "not responding" is it?

Before you fix anything, work out what is actually happening. Does the screen ignore every touch, or only taps in one area? Does it work for a few seconds after you unlock it, then freeze? Is one app stuck while the home button or gesture still works? A screen that is dead everywhere points to the phone or the screen itself. A screen that only fails inside one app points to that app. And a screen that taps the wrong place, or types letters by itself, is usually a damaged or dirty screen, or a cheap charger interfering with it while it is plugged in.

1. Clean the screen and dry your hands

Turn the screen off first, then wipe it with a clean, slightly damp microfibre cloth — the kind that comes with glasses. Water, grease, food and hand cream all sit on the glass and confuse the touch sensor, which works by detecting the tiny electrical charge in your fingertip. Dry the screen properly afterwards, and dry your hands too: wet fingers and gloves all make touches unreliable, because ordinary gloves do not conduct, so the screen cannot feel them.

2. Remove the case and the screen protector

Take the phone out of its case, and peel off the screen protector if you can do it without cracking the screen further. A case that presses on the edges of the screen can cause phantom touches, and a thick, bubbled or cracked protector can block touches in one area — especially near the edges. Test the screen with both removed. If it works again, the protector was the problem: put on a new, properly fitted one rather than going without. If the screen itself is cracked, be careful of sharp glass, and remember that a cracked screen can keep working for weeks and then fail suddenly — that is a repair, not a setting.

3. Force-restart the phone

If the screen is frozen, a normal restart is impossible because you cannot tap anything — so use the button combination instead. On most Android phones, hold the power button for about 20 to 30 seconds until the phone vibrates and restarts. On iPhone, press and quickly release volume up, press and quickly release volume down, then hold the side button until the Apple logo appears. A force restart does not delete anything; it just clears the temporary state that is freezing the screen. After it restarts, test the screen in a simple place, like the lock screen or the settings menu, before opening the app that was running when it froze.

4. If one app is the problem, fix the app

If the screen works everywhere except in one app, that app is crashing, not the phone. Close it completely — on Android, open the recent apps view and swipe it away; on iPhone, swipe up from the bottom and swipe the app away — then open it again. If it freezes again, update it from the Play Store or App Store, because the update may fix exactly this bug. If there is no update, clear the app's cache: on Android, go to Settings, then Apps, pick the app, tap Storage, then Clear cache. Do not tap Clear data by mistake — that logs you out and deletes the app's saved content. If the app still freezes, delete it and reinstall it, after checking that your data is backed up or saved in your account.

5. Check for software and hardware causes

If the whole screen is still dead after a restart, check two things. First, is the phone's software up to date? Go to Settings, then Software update on Android, or General, then Software Update on iPhone, and install any update — touch problems are sometimes fixed in updates. Second, think about damage: did the phone get wet or get dropped? Water inside the phone and a loose screen connector after a drop both cause dead areas, and neither is fixed by software. Also unplug the charger and test again — a faulty or very cheap charger can make the touchscreen jump and tap by itself while charging. If a whole strip never responds, that is almost certainly hardware, and the next step is a repair shop.

Common mistakes to avoid

The biggest mistake is factory-resetting the phone at the first freeze — that deletes everything and rarely fixes a dirty screen or a bad protector, so try the cheap fixes first and back up before you reset anything. Another is pressing harder and harder on a dead screen, which can crack it further. A third is using a wet cloth dripping with water, or a cleaning spray, on the phone — water in the speaker or charging port causes more damage than the dirt did.

Quick checklist

Clean the screen and dry your hands, remove the case and screen protector and test again, then force-restart the phone with the buttons. If only one app freezes, close it, update it, or clear its cache. If a whole area stays dead, take it for repair — and back up your data first if you still can.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my touchscreen work sometimes and not others? Usually a dirty screen, wet hands, a bad screen protector, or an app freezing. If it fails in the same area every time, that area of the screen may be damaged.

Can a screen protector really stop the screen working? Yes — a thick, bubbled or badly fitted protector can block touches, especially near the edges. Peel it off and test the bare screen before assuming the phone is broken.

How do I know if it needs a repair? If the screen is cracked, was dropped or got wet, or a whole strip never responds after a restart and an update, it is hardware. Back up what you can and take it to a repair shop.
