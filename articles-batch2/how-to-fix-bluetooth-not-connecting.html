How to Fix Bluetooth That Won't Connect

Bluetooth should just work, and when it does not, it is frustrating precisely because the fix is usually small. Work through these steps in order, and test the connection after each one, so you stop as soon as the problem is solved instead of changing ten things at once.

Why Bluetooth connections fail

Bluetooth is a short-range radio connection, and it fails for a handful of predictable reasons: the device is still paired to another phone or laptop, it is out of range or blocked by a wall, its battery is low, or the pairing information on one side has gone stale. Software updates, especially on phones, can also reset Bluetooth permissions or break a driver. The steps below cover all of these causes, starting with the fastest checks and ending with a clean re-pair, which fixes almost everything that remains.

1. Check the basics: power, range, and battery

First, make sure the Bluetooth device is actually on and charged. Many headphones and speakers turn themselves off after a few minutes of inactivity, and a low battery can cause pairing to fail even when the device appears to switch on. Move the device within a metre of your phone or laptop, with no thick wall between them. Bluetooth range is officially around ten metres, but in a real home full of walls, Wi-Fi routers, and other wireless devices, the reliable range is much shorter. If it connects at close range, distance or interference was the problem, not a broken device.

2. Turn Bluetooth off and on again on both devices

On your phone or laptop, turn Bluetooth off, wait ten seconds, and turn it back on. Do the same on the accessory if it has a power switch. This clears the short-term state of the Bluetooth radio on both sides, and it fixes a surprising number of failures caused by a glitch after waking from sleep. Do not skip the ten-second wait: switching it off and straight back on sometimes does not give the radio time to fully reset. After both sides are back on, try connecting again before moving to the next step.

3. Restart both devices

If toggling Bluetooth did not help, restart your phone or laptop and the accessory. A restart clears stuck Bluetooth services and drivers, which is the most common cause of a connection that worked yesterday and fails today. This is especially worth trying after a system update, because updates can leave a Bluetooth driver in a half-loaded state until the next restart. Power the accessory fully off rather than just putting it to sleep, then power it on and try again.

4. Forget the device and pair it again

Stale pairing data is the most common cause of a device that shows up but refuses to connect. On your phone or laptop, open Bluetooth settings, find the device, and choose Forget or Remove device. Then put the accessory into pairing mode, usually by holding its power or Bluetooth button until a light flashes, and pair it fresh. This creates a brand-new connection with new keys, and it resolves problems where the device is still secretly connected to another phone in the house. Remember to check that other phone: if your headphones are connected to your tablet, they may refuse your laptop until you disconnect them there.

5. Check for interference from Wi-Fi and USB devices

Bluetooth and Wi-Fi both use the 2.4 GHz band, and a busy Wi-Fi network can crowd Bluetooth out. If problems happen mainly near your router, try moving away from it, or switch your Wi-Fi to the 5 GHz band if your router offers it. USB 3 devices, including external hard drives and hubs, are another known source of interference, particularly on laptops: moving a USB 3 drive away from the laptop's Bluetooth antenna can restore a flaky connection. Wireless mice and keyboards using their own USB receivers can also compete, so test with them unplugged if the problem persists.

6. Update drivers and system software

On Windows, open Device Manager, expand Bluetooth, right-click your Bluetooth adapter, and choose Update driver. Also check Windows Update for any pending updates. On a Mac, Bluetooth drivers arrive with macOS updates, so install those instead. On Android and iPhone, install the latest system update available. Manufacturers fix Bluetooth bugs regularly, and a device that stopped working after an update is often fixed by the next one. If your PC uses a separate Bluetooth dongle, check the dongle maker's website for a driver, since Windows Update does not always find the newest version.

7. Reset network settings as a last resort

If nothing else works, resetting network settings on a phone will clear all Bluetooth pairings, saved Wi-Fi networks, and VPN settings, and give Bluetooth a clean start. On Android, this is under Settings, then System, then Reset options, then Reset Wi-Fi, mobile and Bluetooth. On iPhone, it is under Settings, then General, then Transfer or Reset iPhone, then Reset, then Reset Network Settings. This is a bigger step because you will need to re-enter Wi-Fi passwords, so only use it after the earlier steps fail. On Windows, a similar option is Network reset under Settings, then Network and internet, then Advanced network settings.

8. Test with a second device to find the faulty side

Before you conclude that a device is broken, pair it with a different phone or laptop. If it works there, the problem is with your first device's Bluetooth, not the accessory, and a driver update or network reset on that device is the right fix. If it fails everywhere, the accessory itself is likely faulty, and you should check its warranty or try a factory reset using the button combination in its manual. This one test saves a lot of wasted effort, because it tells you which side of the connection to keep troubleshooting.

Final checklist

Check power and range, toggle Bluetooth on both sides, restart both devices, and then forget and re-pair the accessory. Check for Wi-Fi and USB interference, update drivers, and only then reset network settings. Finally, test with a second device to confirm which side is at fault. Most Bluetooth problems are solved by step four, so do not jump to a reset before trying the simple steps first.

Frequently asked questions

Why do my headphones connect to the wrong device? Most Bluetooth accessories reconnect to the last device they were used with. Disconnect them from that device first, or turn off Bluetooth on it, before pairing with a new one.

Can I connect Bluetooth headphones to two devices at once? Only if the headphones support multipoint pairing. Check the product specifications: if multipoint is not listed, the headphones can hold only one active connection at a time.
